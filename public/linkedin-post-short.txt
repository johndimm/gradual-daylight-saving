In 1914, William Willett proposed 8 clock shifts a year instead of 2 — each only 20 minutes. Parliament rejected it. Too many resets.

But Willett was right about the problem: nobody likes losing an hour of sleep.

Today most clocks reset themselves. The cost of a shift is nearly zero.

So follow his logic all the way: 365 shifts of 32 seconds. One per day. You never notice.
